BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW KNOWLEDGE BASE
=====================================

STAR Method (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
The STAR method is the gold standard for answering behavioral questions. Always structure behavioral answers with a clear Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Each component should be specific and concise.

Common Behavioral Question Categories:
1. Leadership and Influence
2. Conflict Resolution
3. Teamwork and Collaboration
4. Problem Solving under Pressure
5. Failure and Learning
6. Time Management and Prioritization
7. Communication and Stakeholder Management

Leadership Questions:
- Tell me about a time you led a team through a difficult project.
- Describe a situation where you had to influence without authority.
- Give an example of when you set a vision and motivated others.
Strong answers demonstrate initiative, clear communication, empathy, and measurable outcomes.

Conflict Resolution:
- Describe a time you disagreed with a teammate or manager.
- How did you handle a situation where two team members were in conflict?
Best answers show active listening, empathy, finding common ground, and preserving relationships while maintaining productivity.

Handling Failure:
- Tell me about a project that failed and what you learned.
- Describe a time you made a mistake at work.
Strong answers show accountability, reflection, root cause analysis, and concrete steps taken to prevent recurrence.

Teamwork:
- Describe your experience working in cross-functional teams.
- How do you handle a team member who is not contributing?
Best answers show collaboration, clear communication, willingness to help, and team-oriented mindset.

Time Management:
- How do you prioritize tasks when everything is urgent?
- Describe a time you managed multiple deadlines simultaneously.
Strong answers demonstrate structured prioritization (e.g., Eisenhower matrix), delegation skills, and tool use (e.g., Jira, Trello).

Adaptability:
- Tell me about a time you had to adapt to major change at work.
- Describe a situation where requirements changed midway through a project.
Best answers show flexibility, positive attitude toward change, quick learning, and continued delivery under uncertainty.

Evaluation Criteria for Behavioral Answers:
- Specificity (real example vs vague generality)
- Clarity of impact and result
- Self-awareness and ownership
- Communication clarity
- Alignment with company values

Score 9-10: Specific, structured STAR, quantified impact, deep self-reflection
Score 7-8: Clear STAR, good impact, minor gaps in reflection
Score 5-6: Partial STAR, some specifics, limited impact or reflection
Score 3-4: Vague answer, missing key components, minimal structure
Score 1-2: No real example, evasive or unrelated answer
